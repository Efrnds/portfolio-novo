## Product intent

- Purpose: present Eduardo as a full-stack engineer who also understands deployment and day-to-day operations.

- Primary action: inspect shipped systems, then start a conversation.

## Findings resolved in the audit pass

| ID | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A-01 | Medium | The carousel used 40 px controls on small screens. | Navigation, close, and slide controls now provide at least 44 px hit areas. |
| A-02 | Medium | The closed off-canvas menu could leave controls in the keyboard order. | The dialog only mounts while open, receives initial focus, traps Tab, closes with Escape, and restores focus. |
| A-03 | Medium | The hero map and playground flow initially compressed their desktop rows on narrow screens. | Both reflow into vertical, readable sequences below 768 px. |
| A-04 | Medium | Dragging a carousel slide could also trigger the lightbox click. | Drag intent is tracked separately and cancels the following click. |
| A-05 | Low | A generic image gradient remained in the carousel. | Replaced with a functional solid control rail. |
| A-06 | Medium | The first chapter pass still reused one visual shell across all featured projects. | Rebuilt each case around a project-specific artifact: Trato operating console, Opinioteca feed/message/mobile network, and Calmera production ledger with storefront diptych. |

## Pattern review

- No generic 3D ornament, floating geometry, glow, glass surface, decorative gradient, or dashboard-style card wall remains.
- The orange accent has one job: system state, sequence markers, focus, and interaction feedback.
- Motion is finite and supports entry, state change, or navigation. Reduced-motion preferences remove nonessential duration.
- Project repetition is limited to the shared heading and evidence grammar. Trato, Opinioteca, and Calmera use different spatial models, image treatments, reading orders, and project-color cues.
- Copy describes operating context and outcomes instead of generic craft claims.
- Stack names appear as supporting evidence, not as the homepage's main content.
